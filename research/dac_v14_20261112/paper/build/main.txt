Reachability-Constrained Shared-Crossing Planning
for 3D FPGA Routing
Anonymous submission draft
Abstract—V ertical connectivity complicates timing-driven
FPGA routing because feasible crossings differ across sinks
and architectures. We develop a reachability-constrained planner
that compares shared and split crossing assignments on a ﬁnite
terminal hierarchy, then softly guides VTR routing. Controlled
experiments separate routing, placement, and toolchain effects
across three architectures. On complete dual-baseline cohorts of
two to four circuits, timing changes range from a 0.64% regres-
sion to a 1.32% improvement; larger individual gains coexist with
regressions and failures. All ten valid serial timing groups incur
overhead. Mechanism analysis exposes sparse delay coverage
and motivates a controlled test of the planner’s independent
contribution.
Index Terms —3D FPGA, detailed routing, vertical intercon-
nect, dynamic programming, architecture exploration
I. I NTRODUCTION
Stacking FPGA layers changes the routing decisions that
translate architectural resources into performance. A net may
share vertical crossings among branches or select separate
crossings for critical sinks. Nearby resources may be un-
reachable through the directed routing resource graph (RRG),
and reachable choices may become poor after congestion
negotiation or a critical-path change.
Recent tools enable detailed exploration of three-
dimensional fabrics. VTR supports 3D architectures, LaZagna
generates ﬂexible vertical connectivity, and Escaping Flatland
improves the placement ﬂow used to assess these fabrics [1],
[2], [3], [4]. These advances make it possible to ask a
narrower routing question: with the packed netlist, placement,
architecture, and timing model ﬁxed, does coordinating cross-
ing choices across a net improve its ﬁnal routed timing?
Answering this question requires distinguishing a routing
modiﬁcation from the beneﬁt of a different placement or a
different toolchain.
We investigate a planner that represents legal crossing
choices explicitly and compares sharing against splitting
within a deterministic hierarchy of remote terminals. The plan-
ner combines directed reachability, spatial candidate reduction,
criticality ﬁltering, and optional bounded delay queries. Its
output is a soft preference used by the existing detailed
router. Feasible resources remain available, and the ﬁnal route
is judged by the ordinary routing and timing checks. The
implementation is based on a ﬁxed VTR 9 development
revision; it is not a new fabric generator or placer.
Our contributions are a ﬁnite shared-crossing objective
with explicit fallback behavior; ﬁxed-input evaluation across
switch-block (SB), output connection-box (CB-O), and hybrid
(Hybrid-O) connectivity, separating two placement ﬂows and
three baseline identities; and mechanism analysis connecting
improvements and regressions to coverage, ﬁnal resources,
changing worst paths, and runtime costs.
The results support conditional timing effects, not a general
performance victory. On the complete cohorts used for the
paired 3D/2D display, mean routing improvements range from
−0.64% to 1.32%, with only two to four circuits contributing
to each mean. A single Hybrid-O case improves by 12.96%,
while another worsens by 11.45%. All ten serial groups with
three legal paired repetitions are slower than the native base-
line. These outcomes make coverage and attribution central to
the contribution: a resource-efﬁcient route or a better isolated
example does not establish lower critical-path delay across the
suite.
II. R ELATED WORK
VTR provides FPGA architecture and CAD infrastruc-
ture [8]; version 9 added 3D support [1]. The 3D extensions
studied by Boutros et al. connect device models with CAD-
level exploration [5]. We use that infrastructure and retain its
detailed router, timing analysis, and legality checks. The new
element is a crossing-selection layer above detailed connection
search, with a deliberately limited optimization objective.
LaZagna supports architecture generation and evaluation
beyond a single vertical-connectivity template [3]. Its original
published VTR 8.1 results and the later VTR 9-code port are
different baselines. Neither is interchangeable with the ofﬁcial-
source VTR derivative used here. Escaping Flatland addresses
partitioning, delay estimation, annealing costs, and moves dur-
ing placement [4]; our routing comparison instead holds each
resulting placement ﬁxed. Its architecture-ranking observations
motivate checking rankings, rather than predicting that another
routing method must reproduce a reversal.
H3D optimizes bonding-terminal number and positions for
face-to-face IC global routing [9]. We borrow the shared-
crossing idea, not its geometric model or guarantees. Our ﬁnite
hierarchy handles directed, prefabricated FPGA resources; it
is neither H3D’s algorithm nor a global FPGA Steiner-tree
solver. Dynamic programming and strongly connected com-
ponents are established tools, not standalone novelty claims.
III. P ROBLEM MODEL
Let G = ( V, E) be a directed RRG. Each resource v has
capacity uv, physical location and layer, and an associated
routing cost. Edges identify actual connections and switches.
For each net n, source sn and terminals Tn are ﬁxed by a
packed netlist and placement. Detailed routing selects con-
nected legal trees subject to shared capacities, while timing
analysis evaluates the resulting circuit. We restrict the planner
to two layers and clustered, serial routing.
A crossing candidate c denotes a real vertical entry resource,
not a freely placeable via. In the evaluated edge encoding, SB
crossings use paired half-nodes and CB-O crossings originate
at an output pin. Different CB edges can share the same
output resource; counting every edge as an independent via
would overstate capacity. A native CHANZ representation is
supported separately, but is not silently equated with these
edge-encoded resources.
For a remote terminal i, candidate c is feasible when a
same-layer directed path reaches its entry from sn and an
actual crossing exit can reach i on the destination layer. These
conditions certify a single-crossing walk in the full graph.
They do not certify a congestion-free route for all nets, a route
inside the current search bounding box, or the best possible
multi-crossing route. Reverse traversal is an analysis operation
only; it never adds edges to the router.
Let Ci be the ﬁnite candidate table available to terminal
i, and let αi be its routing criticality at initial planning. We
use an opening proxy o(c) for source access and prospective
resource congestion, and a branch proxy bi(c) for sink access
and criticality. A representative form is
o(c) = ds(c) + λoac[1 + p max(0, qc − uc)], (1)
bi(c) = (1 + 4 αi)di(c) + 4αids(c). (2)
Here ac is accumulated congestion cost, p is the present-cost
factor, and qc includes one prospective unit unless the net
already owns the resource. Distances are normalized geometry
proxies or supported switch-delay estimates. Partial delay
coverage can mix measured opening cost and geometric branch
cost.

IV. M ETHOD
A. Directed feasibility and candidate reduction
We query the strongly connected component condensation
of same-layer edges, preserving directed reachability. Spatial
sampling considers endpoints, interpolated locations, and the
net centroid. Feasible candidates are ranked by their proxy
costs and diversiﬁed across sites; feasible crossings already
owned by the net remain eligible. If the spatial sample provides
no feasible candidate, the planner scans the crossing table for
a usable fallback set. Current occupancy does not by itself
exclude a resource, because later congestion negotiation may
release it.
The pooled candidates from all eligible terminals form the
ﬁnite DP table. Each terminal is checked against this pool
again, and entries with unavailable measured branch cost
are omitted from the surrogate. A candidate introduced by
one terminal can serve another. The proposed independent-
selection ablation must retain this same table.
B. Finite shared/split planning
Terminals are recursively divided at the median along the
larger spatial extent. At each hierarchy node, the recurrence
compares one common crossing with the two child solutions.
Induction over the hierarchy proves optimality within these
alternatives: each allowed partition either shares at the current
node or consists of allowed child partitions. This statement
does not cover other hierarchies, excluded candidates, or
global capacity coupling. Ties favor a shared group, then
deterministic candidate order. Nets with more than 128 eligible
remote terminals use independent candidate guidance and
increment a fallback counter.
C. Budgeted delay scoring and routing integration
Delay queries perform bounded exploration and cache end-
point results. The implementation accepts its additive switch-
delay proxy only for its supported zero-wire-RC model. A
truncated search is not an infeasibility proof. Fully delay-
scored terminal choices require enough candidates covered by
both forward and reverse searches; otherwise geometric terms
remain available. We report query, truncation, cache-hit, and
fully scored-terminal counts separately.
The selected conﬁguration uses a static guide: each net
is planned once, and the guide is reused on later reroutes.
Unpreferred crossing resources receive a nonnegative extra
congestion preference, while existing timing-driven search and
congestion penalties remain active. A failed guided sink search
retries without the guide before ordinary recovery. Static
caching bounds repeated planning but may preserve an early
decision after congestion or criticality changes. This tradeoff
is part of the method, not a claim that all later routing states
are optimized.
D. Selective target feedback (development candidate)
The independently developed candidate Tf retains the frozen
R1 initial plan and cost integration, but permits selective guide
refresh. VPR ﬁrst selects a net for native rerouting. Before
pruning, we capture its crossing occupancy and actual crossing
set; after pruning, VPR supplies the remaining-target set Tn. A
refresh requires a three-iteration cooldown and either crossing
overuse, eligible criticality drift of at least 0.15, or crossing-
set Jaccard distance of at least 0.25. Criticality drift remains a
net-level trigger; restricting that trigger to targets is a separate,
unselected ablation.
The new ﬁnite table contains only eligible remote terminals
in Tn. Static SCC information and valid distance results are
reused; congestion prices and candidate assignment are recom-
puted. Both the old and proposed assignments are evaluated on
the current table with the same shared/split objective. For ﬁnite
old cost, a ﬁnite proposal is accepted only if Jnew < 0.95Jold.
An unavailable old assignment may be replaced by a ﬁnite
one; absence of any eligible target instead permits deactivation
of target guides. This exception is an eligibility update, not
a cost-improvement claim. Guides and recorded criticalities
outside Tn remain unchanged. A membership bitmap makes
this preservation check linear in the number of sinks plus
targets.
Accepted guides participate in the existing routing transac-
tion and best-route snapshots, so recovery restores the associ-
ated guidance state. The method does not force a retained
branch to reroute, alter physical switch delays, or replace
native congestion negotiation or ﬁnal STA. Reducing the active
terminal set can also bring a high-fanout net below the existing
128-terminal shared-DP limit; this is part of the change, not
evidence that every gain comes from avoiding updates to re-
tained branches. Tf remains a development candidate pending
broader quality, failure, and controlled runtime validation.
V. E XPERIMENTAL SETUP
A. Tools, architectures, and comparison identities
The native baseline A0 and our router R1 derive from
ofﬁcial VTR development commit 0b912334; R1 adds the
planner and graph-encoding integration. Strict serial builds
have separate recorded binary identities. B2 is the modern
LaZagna port at VTR commit 5abd3aca [7], with archived
build and compatibility changes. It has been built and exercised
under controlled inputs. Its historical version string is not used
to infer equivalence with ofﬁcial VTR 9. The original LaZagna
VTR 8.1 paper is B1, an external published reference rather
than a matched local routing experiment.
We use derived SB, CB-O, and Hybrid-O architectures
with a common planar graph and segment mix. V ertical con-
nections retain their generated directions and switch timing.
Import checks compare directed connections, switches, and
resource semantics between source and native representations.
Matching logical capacity does not imply equal area, power,
or vertical-interconnect budgets. The modeled wires have
zero R/C while switch delays remain; these are architecture
experiments, not calibrated silicon predictions.
For each circuit, the registered sizing rule selects the
smallest permitted grid side satisfying at most 80% occu-
pancy for every logical block class. A common size is used
across architectures and placement ﬂows. The matched 2D
reference preserves logical capacities with a separate near-
square layout. Its planar wire-unit inventory differs from 3D
by −0.18% to +1.87% across the prepared models. It is
an architecture/placement denominator, not a ﬁxed-placement
routing control. Main measurements use width 100 and ideal
clocks, a maximum of 200 routing iterations, disabled failure
prediction, a 1800-second route-process limit and a 3600-
second placement limit. A 64-GiB address-space cap applies
per solver.
B. Placement, selection, and evaluation separation
P0 is native placement on the paper-packed netlist. P1 exe-
cutes the paper placement ﬂow, including weighted TritonPart
initialization, and repacks to populate criticality information.
Packed-netlist semantics are checked against the census. Every
A0/R1/B2 routing contrast within a ﬂow uses private copies
of the same packed netlist and placement coordinates, with
equivalent graph and timing inputs. A P1/P0 contrast changes
placement and setup work and is reported separately.
Six training circuits use seed 11: attention_layer, bnn,
eltwise_layer, gemm_layer, softmax, and spmv. Fourteen other
circuits use evaluation seeds 21, 23, and 25. These circuits
had appeared in historical development experiments; the split
is fresh for this selection exercise, not evidence of previously
unseen benchmark families. Each of four variants—geometry
(G), delay (D), criticality (C), and their combination (D+C)—
receives eight registered conﬁgurations per architecture. All
four retain the shared/split DP . Selection prioritizes legal cov-
erage and then circuit-balanced timing on comparable training
cases; selected conﬁgurations are frozen before evaluation.

B. Runtime cost and architecture ordering
All ten R1/A0 groups with three legal serial repetitions
incur router overhead, with median ratios from 1.129 to
3.382. The corresponding route-process ratios are 1.047–
1.313. Two robot_rl/CB-O groups lack legal paired repetitions
and remain missing rather than neutral. Observed median
setup spans 13.59–71.25 s, including 0.84–6.04 s of partition
work where applicable. P0 reuses census packing whereas P1
repacks; these are ﬂow-speciﬁc costs, not equal-work end-to-
end speedups.
The same-build R1-off control also shows overhead in all
ten complete groups: R1/R1-off spans 1.180–3.327 for router
time and 1.015–1.334 for process time. Against B2, these
ranges are 0.906–4.214 and 0.910–1.267; only one and two
groups, respectively, are faster. These toolchain differences do
not establish overall acceleration.
Only conv_layer_hls belongs to the complete intersection
across all three architectures and both routing/placement
choices. On this circuit’s three-seed means, all four combina-
tions rank CB-O, then SB, then Hybrid-O; the strict reversal
count is zero. Individual seed rankings change, however: P1
ranks SB ﬁrst at seed 21 and Hybrid-O ﬁrst at seed 25, for both
A0 and R1. With this limited intersection, neither a general
best architecture nor a ranking reversal is supported. Architec-

VII. M ECHANISM ANALYSIS
A. Coverage is narrower than the algorithm’s name
The frozen CB-O threshold of 0.95 selects few remote
terminals. Across its legal main runs, the mean selected-
terminal fraction is 0.142% for P0 and 0.0143% for P1; all
legal R1/A0 CPDs tie. Sparse guide application is consistent
with this lack of a ﬁnal timing effect, but the counters alone
do not establish that a lower threshold would help. A broader
guide can also increase work or redirect routing adversely.
Hybrid-O enables delay estimation, yet complete for-
ward/reverse coverage is rare. Its legal P0 runs fully score
32 of 44,064 eligible terminals in aggregate; P1 scores 60
of 43,119. All 32,941 P0 and 46,650 P1 recorded endpoint
queries hit their exploration cap. Truncation does not imply
an empty query: settled entries remain useful, and measured
source costs can affect choices that fall back for branch
scoring. The data expose a mismatch between the conﬁgured
budget and the candidate sets, rather than proving that delay-
aware optimization is intrinsically ineffective.

B. An improvement with a different critical path
For clstm_like.small, the planner selects 530 of 3902 remote
terminals, forms 372 groups, and records no empty-candidate,
high-fanout, or guided-search fallback events. The ﬁnal route
uses thirteen fewer SB pairs but ﬁve more CB crossing output
sources. Planar length falls by only 74 units, or about 0.033%.
The observed CPD gain therefore accompanies a change in
route topology rather than a large reduction in total wirelength.
The saved A0 worst path runs through a multiplier in an
inverse-transform block. Under R1, the worst path instead lies
in an elementwise subtraction block; the start and end registers
both change. Its reduced worst delay shows that the overall
routing perturbation improves this sample. However, the two
saved reports describe different paths, so their component
delays cannot be subtracted as if they measured the same
connection. In particular, they do not identify which chosen
crossing relieved the original worst path. A connection-level
causal account would require matched path traces or an
additional instrumented control.
Only two eligible terminals receive complete delay scoring.
The observed improvement consequently supports the whole
selected conﬁguration, not a claim that the bounded delay
estimator or DP alone caused it. Planning consumes 28.73
seconds out of 34.78 reported routing seconds, approximately
83%. The same 26 negotiated-congestion iterations do not
imply equal work per iteration. Caching removes repeated
planning but does not remove its initial cost.
C. A degradation despite similar resource use
For clstm_like.large, CPD increases from 7.07372 to
7.88347 ns. The ﬁnal route uses one additional SB pair,
three fewer CB crossing sources, and 226 more planar-length
units, about 0.036%. These small changes in aggregate re-
sources fail to predict the 11.45% timing degradation. The
worst path changes from one multiplier/register chain to a
different inverse-transform multiplier path. The saved reports
again support a bottleneck change, not a matched-edge delay
decomposition.
The planner forms 938 groups from 1233 eligible terminals,
but only two receive complete delay scoring. It records no
guided-search, high-fanout, or empty-candidate fallback. Thus
fallback activation is not an explanation for this regression. A
feasible, cached guide can simply steer congestion negotiation
toward a worse ﬁnal critical path. Planning takes 106.13 of
126.24 router seconds, approximately 84%, and routing needs
29 iterations instead of 24. This case exposes both proxy
mismatch and substantial setup cost.
Pairing ﬁnal connections by net name, pin index, and
identical SOURCE/SINK resource IDs yields an additional
distinction. In the large case, mean switch delay across all
10,863 remote connections falls from 1.92973 to 1.87781 ns
(2.69%): 3485 improve, 4617 tie, and 2761 regress, despite
worse CPD. The small case has 626/2735/541 outcomes across
3902 remote connections, with a 1.65% mean improvement.
These audited zero-RC switch sums exclude local logic de-
lays and do not isolate a planner component. Better average
interconnect quality can coexist with a worse worst path.
D. What ﬁnal resource counters establish
Independent route audits establish the ﬁnal SB-pair and CB-
source occupancies reported above. The guided_paths and
preferred_paths logs instead count routing events across
iterations, including reroutes. For the favorable case their ﬁnal
values are 3657 and 2023; they are not a 55% ﬁnal-route
adoption rate. No frozen per-connection ﬁnal-guide trace is
available to compute that rate. We therefore distinguish ﬁnal
resource use, cumulative guide events, and causal resource
selection. Their identities must not be inferred from similar
aggregate totals.
VIII. S ENSITIVITY , ATTRIBUTION , AND LIMITATIONS
A. Scoring ablations and partition balance
The four scoring variants are compared with conﬁguration 0
ﬁxed and with equal eight-conﬁguration training budgets. On
the joint P1/seed-21 Hybrid-O cohort of four circuits, ﬁxed
G, D, C, and D+C improve CPD over A0 by 5.63%, 4.25%,
0.38%, and 0.38%, respectively. After separate selection, the
values are 5.63%, 2.51%, −1.89%, and 3.29%. Individual
legal coverage differs: selected D has 4/14 legal pairs and
selected D+C has 8/14. The common-cohort mean and broader
coverage describe different properties. These results do not
support monotonic beneﬁt from adding scoring terms, and all
arms still contain DP .
A registered balance sensitivity study compares literal parti-
tion argument 0.03 with a relative-3% interpretation, argument
1.5 for two equal target partitions. Across four circuits, three
architectures, two widths, two placement arms, and three
routing roles, 130 of 144 roles are legal, 12 fail, and two time
out. Independent weight audits satisfy their respective bounds

B. DP attribution in the frozen evaluation
The current candidates mode is not an adequate DP-off
control. It changes guide lifetime and retains multiple indepen-
dent preferences, while the DP commonly assigns a singleton.
The frozen v13 evaluation did not execute an independent top-
1 control. The post-registration development experiment below
implements two arms sharing candidate sampling, the pooled
feasible table, scores, delay queries, congestion state, and
static cache lifetime. Arm I selects each terminal’s minimum
opening-plus-branch-cost candidate independently; Arm S ap-
plies shared/split DP to the same table. Both produce singleton
guides on the supported DP population. Existing high-fanout
and empty-table fallback behavior remains common.
The isolated development snapshot adds one selector after
table construction. V alidation must reproduce frozen DP-on
routes and CPDs, verify both solvers on enumerated small
tables, and check cache behavior. First-plan table hashes verify
equality; later trajectory differences are guidance outcomes.
The proposed diagnostic pilot uses spmv/SB/P0/seed11,

The registered ﬁxed-placement diagnostic completed all
24 cases and 144 roles: 125 legal, 13 failed, and 6 timed
out. For selected R1, 19 of 24 four-route width interac-

D. Limits of inference and implications for exploration
Dual-baseline cohorts cover only two to four of four-
teen circuits. Architecture-dependent missingness prevents all-
suite claims; three seeds do not establish broad signiﬁcance,
and prior exposure limits independence. Logical-capacity and
planar-graph matching do not equalize silicon area, power,
or via budgets. Ideal clocks and zero-wire-RC models limit
technology-level inference.
Mechanistic uncertainty also remains. The shared/split re-
currence is implemented and independently checkable, but the
present scoring ablations do not isolate its contribution. Sparse
delay-query coverage, a ﬁxed hierarchy, candidate truncation,
static criticalities, and lack of global congestion coupling
can all limit the surrogate. Candidate-table reuse and more
targeted delay queries are plausible optimization directions;
they were not measured in the frozen evaluation, and the
separate development study below tests them. The strict timing
results already rule out describing the current implementation
as a general acceleration.
Architecture exploration should report legality, timing, re-
source use, and cost together. A few improved samples cannot
establish a general architecture ranking; no reversal is an
acceptable outcome.

J. Persistent endpoint frontiers and exact closure integration
The E strategy still rebuilds an endpoint-side frontier for
each named candidate. A separate P strategy retains that
frontier while creating a new opposite frontier for each target.
Reusing the old single-target-pruned graph would be invalid
when the target changes. P therefore includes all nonexpanding
crossing terminals from the beginning, so the graph remains
immutable across requests. Every newly settled endpoint-side
node, including an incidental terminal, and every opposite-
side node is charged to the same lifetime 4,096-settlement
endpoint/direction budget; the per-request quota remains 256.
An eight-state LRU retains frontiers, with exact-answer caches
and work ledgers surviving eviction. Restarted work is charged
again. An active query limits combined labels and heap entries
to 200,000 and 500,000, respectively; container overhead and
other graph storage remain subject to the process guard. A
dropped endpoint-side relaxation poisons that saved frontier,
preventing unsafe resumption. This is a different query strat-
egy, not a claim of decision-equivalent bookkeeping optimiza-
tion.

A. Problem and mechanism
The next independent development batches retain strict CPD
improvement as one mode, but do not require every retained
ﬁnal candidate to beat strong A0 in CPD. Two complementary
questions are posed: can timing improve within registered
resource limits, and can routed wirelength or actual vertical
occupancy decrease within a registered timing range? Fixed
CPD, wirelength, SB-pair and CB-source limits are design
parameters anchored at the original batch state. They are
not rolling tolerances or research termination conditions. The
current already-registered observations and their historical
acceptance rules remain unchanged.
The proposed combined intervention uses measured ca-
pacity competition to select noncritical owners of scarce re-
sources, uses their timing margin to reconnect them elsewhere
in a transaction copy, and then repairs critical or near-critical
connections. Occupancy is updated after every step; native
FULL, physical resource accounting and complete STA govern
the ﬁnal commit or rollback. A temporary release need not
independently improve CPD. Final non-dominated retention
is distinct from intermediate search transitions. At present the
ﬁxed-budget policy arithmetic and historical-state replay are
implemented; the new capacity-release mechanism has not yet
produced a new native result.

A. Strong baseline outcome and endpoint tail
The ﬁrst paired combined transactions from pristine strong
A0 have completed native FULL/STA and independent phys-

setup components improve and ten regress; no hold violation
worsens. Strict CPD and the registered timing-under-resources
policy accept, whereas endpoint-vector and wire-reduction
policies reject. The largest setup regression is approximately
557.48 ps. All ten regressed endpoints remain at least 1.896 ns
above the current worst slack and none enters the current 0.2
ns near-critical set. These regressions remain reported rather
than being erased by the CPD gain.
The physical-delay plus hard-capacity pair reaches exactly
the same CPD, but adds 172 wire units, ﬁve SB pairs and

outcome again supplies no quality advantage for that objec-

does not isolate hard capacity as a single factor. Remaining
strong-baseline scope controls continue under the original
registration.
The states express resource tradeoffs: A0 lies between R1
and Tf in CB occupancy while also lying between them in
wirelength and SB use. R1 reaches this CPD through two
direct repairs without releasing owners. Thus owner release
is useful for the demonstrated Tf state but is not necessary
for every starting state. This is a descriptive joint frontier with
different repair scopes, not a whole-algorithm superiority or
area/power claim.
B. From exhaustive reference to automatic proposal
The initial small reference tests every existing candidate
subset: eight each for Tf/R1 and sixteen for A0. A new DAG
dynamic program aggregates winning timing paths by their
candidate-action bit masks, preserving exact path multiplicities
and counting a physical action only once when it maps
to multiple atom nodes. It agrees with all 32 exhaustive
proﬁles. A 121-node synthetic diamond DAG with 240 paths
compresses to two action patterns; coverage and duplicate-
action checks pass. Candidate-action pattern counts can still
grow exponentially, so this is not a polynomial guarantee in
the number of actions.
A bounded branch-and-bound selector retains a ﬁxed frac-
tion of the best current-path gain proxy and minimizes the
union of target and owner repair connections. Twelve actual-
state/fraction comparisons agree with exhaustive selection;
donor-overlap ties and a time-guard case are also checked. If
the per-call guard triggers, it preserves a feasible incumbent
and explicitly withholds proxy optimality. These are process
protections, not research termination conditions. Proxy gains
omit post-repair competition and new critical paths, and do
not guarantee ﬁnal CPD.
The current-state planner now reads certiﬁed current STA,
direct and protected-owner query feedback, derives donor
branches from actual trees, and constructs a native request.
At a uniform 0.9 fraction it reproduces Tf’s 13-connection
group and A0’s 14-connection group. The accepted Tf suc-
cessor explicitly has no positive cover in this proposal class.
Request construction is byte-identical to the already queued
Tf experiment, so that validation creates no duplicate native
job. The smaller-scope native outcomes remain pending.
Original design caps and current transition progress are
distinct in the new multi-round interface. Six existing ﬁrst-step
decisions are unchanged. On actual legal states, moving from
target-only to combined repair improves CPD but increases
wire by eight; it therefore passes the timing policy and fails
resource progress. The reverse transition can reduce resources
within its timing cap while failing strict CPD. Ten checks
preserve legality/transaction gates and constant initial budgets.
These arithmetic checks make no new route commits, and
complete automatic multi-round native integration remains a
separate step.

C. Observed sequential competition and pending-target reser-
vations
The A0 target-only physical arm exposes a concrete coun-
terexample: although legal, it worsens CPD by 178.186798649
ps. Earlier target net 73896 takes three capacity-one nodes
on the original path of later target 73897. Reconstructing
the exact temporary occupancy and own-net retained-tree
credit gives an independent capacity-feasible shortest delay
of 1.2209908917260037 ns, exactly the native result, versus
1.0428040930765548 ns originally. This supports sequential
competition rather than a failed shortest-path calculation for
this event; it is not an impossibility bound on other schedules.
An isolated native version reserves original resources for
unprocessed explicit critical targets, releases its own net’s
reservations during each search, updates occupancy after each
replacement and clears virtual occupancy on both success
and rollback. Donors remain released. A real small native
off/on regression passes unchanged-input, unchanged-original-
STA and occupancy-restoration checks. An initial runtime
failure exposed missing sink indexing on route-ﬁle-loaded
trees and was ﬁxed using VPR’s existing net-bound tree-
copy constructor; failed versions and logs remain preserved.
Large BNN beneﬁt is unknown. A separately registered target-
only/combined by weighted/physical by off/on batch waits
after existing trajectories. Default-off must reproduce old
candidate bodies, and ﬁnal candidates still require independent
physical legality, complete native FULL/STA and exact ﬁxed-
budget decisions. If reservation merely exchanges starvation
for other detours or failures, scheduling or joint allocation
becomes the next discriminating intervention.
XVI. C ONCLUSION
Reachability-constrained shared-crossing planning provides
a concrete way to coordinate vertical choices before detailed
3D FPGA routing. The evaluated static implementation pro-
duces conditional timing improvements and regressions, with
substantial planning overhead and limited successful coverage.
It does not establish general acceleration, universal timing
superiority, or architecture-ranking reversal. The completed
evidence identiﬁes sparse delay coverage and changing crit-
ical paths as central issues, and is complemented by same-
table component ablations, transactional guide feedback, query
controls and exact reuse in the separate development study.
Search-scope controls recover one selected failure even with-
out guides and expose scope-dependent guide regressions,
requiring a stronger matched native baseline. Post-registration
development tests do not yet establish a stable improvement;
the frozen evaluation remains intact.
